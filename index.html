The Unanswered Referral

In June 2017, a GP referred a patient with painful feet, toe clawing and changes in movement. The hospital diagnosed Parkinsonism, but the records raise questions about how the foot problems were assessed, followed up and later reviewed.

Case overview18 November 2026Supporting records available
Open statement · Developing case theory · 25 November 2026The gravest question raised by the recordFrom a warning before discharge to a possible exposure to danger to life.

On 10 July 2017 a patient in a university teaching hospital protested that a material part of the clinical question for which his GP had referred him had not been investigated. By then the hospital had received the referral, narrowed it in its opening summary, recovered important aspects of the omitted history, observed a dystonic-looking movement, initiated neurological treatment and obtained senior advice about levodopa. The original investigation, the completeness of the information supplied to senior colleagues and the educational opportunity were all capable of being examined before the patient left. The protest made the unresolved question explicit.

The patient's gravest allegation is that the response was not to reopen that question but to construct a defensible account of an already determined disposition. He disputes the origin and timing of the handwritten parenthesis “(as the leg pain settled)”, which combines the location from leg tension with the sensation from foot pain. He alleges that it was inserted after his protest, and that its subsequent use in the typed explanation and the emphatic attribution of separation to his own mind helped make a tentative peripheral explanation operational. The completed account omitted his challenge. The date of the parenthesis and the purpose of the construction remain to be established. The senior meeting material must also be obtained: advice given without material information and advice given with it raise different questions of responsibility.

What followed is not a claim that treatment stood still. Treatments changed, and at times brought remarkable relief. The concern is that the underlying framework remained resistant to reconsideration while the patient bore its consequences. As of 25 November 2026, 3,425 days have elapsed since the protest. That is a measure of the age of the unresolved dispute, not a claim of uninterrupted pain or suicidality throughout that period. In March 2023 the GP explicitly reported a death wish; in March 2025 the neurologist recorded “I won’t live with it.” The records examined do not demonstrate the corresponding safety assessments, protection or continuity of follow-up. The fact that the patient survived is not evidence that he was adequately safeguarded.

There is a further possibility that must be examined at the same level of gravity. In April 2018 the neurologist had recorded the patient's report of a rapidly changing symptom at the original outside-left-foot corn site during interruption of procyclidine. In 2021 procyclidine was removed and immediate-release levodopa introduced; the consultant recorded concerns about both undertreatment and excessive movement. The patient alleges that fluctuations between dystonia and dyskinesia, with insufficient overnight coverage, subsequently contributed to his pain. Pregabalin was escalated to 600 mg daily, with conflicting accounts of benefit. The March 2023 death-wish warning and March 2025 declaration of intolerable suffering belong to this very period of treatment, not to a separate history. In March 2025 nighttime controlled-release Sinemet brought profound relief, acknowledged by the neurologist. The question is whether medicine failed to recognise and correct harm to which its own therapeutic arrangements may have contributed, while the patient was communicating distress of potentially life-threatening severity. Medication-related aggravation, inadequate motor control, independent pain mechanisms and other explanations require independent testing. The temporal overlap does not itself establish that either drug caused psychological distress or that the regimen caused the pain. See the canonical clinical record and the essay's treatment-related harm analysis.

The gravest case for independent testing is therefore whether a decision taken in the face of an explicit clinical warning established a durable framework of care that protected an existing professional disposition at the expense of the patient's unresolved needs; whether later evidence was prevented from correcting that framework; whether the therapeutic arrangements themselves contributed to the suffering; and whether the resulting exposure to severe suffering and a foreseeable risk to life was recognised and addressed. The hospital's 2025 no-deficit determination must be examined against those questions, not merely against the rationale for delaying levodopa.

This statement is deliberately open to development. Examine the strongest contrary evidence as actively as the evidence supporting the allegation. Obtain the native handwritten and typed versions, their provenance, the senior meeting material, witness accounts, the clinical and safety records, and the full institutional review. Distinguish the clinical standard, record accuracy, alleged deliberate construction, knowledge, causation, safeguarding and institutional accountability. If the evidence establishes a more serious case, follow it; if it narrows or disproves an allegation, revise it. No private motive, continuous suicidal intent, deliberate concealment, causal contribution to a risk of death or legal liability is treated here as established.
