**Note for counsel | Position reported as at 26 November 2026 | Version 1.0**

This is a dated procedural and evidential briefing, not a finding of infringement, a clinical expert opinion or an assertion of legal liability. It is based on the supplied 26 November correspondence examination. Original correspondence, attachments, receipt records and current status must be checked before formal reliance. Private mailbox links are intentionally omitted from the public repository.

## 1. Why the regulatory proceedings matter to the substantive case

The evidential phase of *The Unanswered Referral* is already underway. The outstanding information is not merely a prospective investigative wish list. The patient has exercised data-subject rights directed at the creation, completeness and use of the very records whose authority is challenged in the substantive case.

The original clinical responsibility, continuing clinical responsibility and institutional review responsibility are independently examinable. The regulatory process provides a possible external means of testing aspects of the information environment underpinning them. As the equal companion essay [Its Defensibility Should Be a Consequence of Its Integrity](integrity-essay.html) explains, the two objects under examination are two distinct exercises of authority: the originating clinical representation and the later institutional determination. The possible double inversion makes the integrity of the independent examination a question in its own right. It does not determine negligence, causation, professional misconduct or the truth of the ward protest.

## 3. Article 15 — the provenance of two exercises of authority

**31 August 2026 — access request; 1 October 2026 — Article 77 complaint.**

The request identifies two information objects:

- **Object A:** July 2017 neurological discharge summary and clinical synthesis.
- **Object B:** 2025 Preliminary Assessment Report (PAR) and Serious Incident Management Team (SIMT) review.

The request seeks existing personal data concerning sourcing, creation, compilation, dictation, transcription, revision, review, approval, finalisation, use and disclosure. The first object established the clinical representation; the second endorsed the adequacy of the care subsequently delivered.

The targeted formulations include the attribution that the patient had markedly divorced his symptoms, the representation that the prolonged admission suited both parties, the claim that the admission provided firmer footing, the patient's attributed understanding of levodopa deferral, and the final adoption of the preceding plan.

If retained draft versions, timestamps, amendments or source information permit comparison before and after the reported 10 July protest, that could materially strengthen, narrow or defeat the deliberate-construction allegation. A comparison is not guaranteed to exist; the request does not presuppose that such drafts survive or that any alteration occurred after the protest. The 2025 object could illuminate whether reviewers independently examined the disputed premises or relied upon them.

The October complaint reports no acknowledgement, processing reference, clarification, extension notice, refusal or substantive response by expiry of the response period. The current absence of a response is reported from the correspondence review, not independently established across all channels.

Article 15 concerns access to personal data, subject to applicable limits; it is not unrestricted litigation discovery or a guarantee of every requested document, draft or internal deliberation. Counsel should consider complementary preservation and disclosure routes.

## 4. Article 16 — completion by supplementary statement

**2 September 2026 — completion request; 5 October 2026 — Article 77 complaint.**

The signed supplementary statement seeks durable association of the patient's account of the July 2017 protest with the historical clinical synthesis, so that its existence is apparent when the synthesis is accessed, relied upon, reviewed or disclosed. It does not seek deletion or rewriting of the clinician's historical account or require acceptance of the patient's testimony as established fact.

The statement records the patient's recollection of four medical students from the local university present behind the curtain in the cubicle when he challenged the adequacy of the foot investigation. These are potential witnesses to professional notice, not established corroborating witnesses. Identifying them and obtaining independent accounts would be a separate evidential exercise.

This request directly addresses the upstream–downstream asymmetry. The author of the clinical account had firsthand knowledge of the admission, whereas subsequent recipients could not recover the omitted protest from that account. Association of the competing statement could make the dispute visible without adjudicating it.

The October complaint records that no determination had been communicated by 2 October. Whether the exact requested method of association is required under Article 16 is a matter for regulatory and legal assessment.

## 5. The significance of the two newer requests

The requests are defined exercises of ordinary data-subject rights, even though the provenance exercise may require technically complex searches. Neither asks the controller to concede negligence or deliberate misconduct. A lawful limitation, refusal or inability to locate data could be explained in a reasoned response.

Their potential evidential consequences are disproportionate to their apparent procedural simplicity. Access could expose or disprove a before-and-after sequence; completion could ensure the original account travels with the patient's recorded objection and notice of potential witnesses. There is a plausible structural conflict between the interests of an institution defending its own records and a data subject seeking to test them. Reported non-response does not prove that the controller wished to conceal evidence or establish any particular motive.

The two newer complaints are expressly outside C-25-7-2074. No separate registration references were located in the supplied review. Their procedural separation must be maintained; exclusion from that reference is not rejection of either complaint.

Investigative priority is not identical to ultimate evidential significance. The comparatively recent PAR may be the most productive immediate disclosure target; corroboration and drafting history could be decisive on the deliberate-construction allegation. Clinical and safeguarding evidence should be pursued in parallel, not deferred until regulatory resolution.

Counsel is invited to advise on preservation of native material, routes to disclosure beyond Article 15, lawful handling of third-party witness information, the interaction between regulatory and other proceedings, and applicable limitation periods or protective steps. Do not assume regulatory proceedings suspend any other deadline.

## 10. The fourth opportunity

The hospital had the opportunity to discover, to correct, and to accept responsibility. It now has the further opportunity to facilitate scrutiny through lawful responses to defined information-rights requests.

The hospital is not being asked to concede the case through those requests. It is being asked to address rights whose exercise could enable the case to be independently tested.
