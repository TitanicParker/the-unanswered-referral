# THE UNANSWERED REFERRAL
## Canonical Working Case — A Documentary Essay in the Words of the Record

**Version:** 0.1.0 — foundational quotation-led draft  
**Prepared:** 19 November 2026  
**Status:** Working reconstruction for independent clinical, documentary and legal examination; **not** an adjudicated finding, a certified transcription, or an authenticated set of native records.  
**Organising principle:** *The clinical state of affairs at the moment the patient protested.*

> **Core proposition for investigation.** On or about 10 July 2017, the patient challenged a clinical state in which the treating neurologist had already identified and begun treating a longstanding motor phenomenon, observed movement somewhat suggestive of dystonic posture, and heard the history of painful and physically abnormal feet, but had not demonstrably resolved how the movement disorder contributed to the clawing, loading, lesions and pain. The completed record nevertheless carried an uncertain peripheral explanation, an emphatic attribution of separation to the patient's own mind, and a management plan culminating in **“As per above.”** The later record tests whether the clinical state challenged at that moment was adequately answered, corrected, handed over, revisited, and independently reviewed.

**Evidence discipline.** This essay is constructed primarily from publicly available *redacted reproductions/transcriptions of clinician-authored records*, an indexed GP transcription, a partly transcribed specialist letter, and a transcribed podiatry record. These reproductions are useful documentary witnesses but are **not independently authenticated native hospital files**. The July manuscript quotations below come from a **working transcription** and must be checked against the photographs. The patient's account of the pre-discharge protest is treated as the **stipulated premise of this analytical exercise** (the patient says it occurred as described), but the signed statement is retrospective testimony, not a contemporaneous hospital entry. Dates of first drafting, the full 2025 PAR/SIMT papers, medication administration clock-times, and several source-delivery records remain unverified. **Quoted punctuation, distinctions between foot pain/leg pain/tension, qualifications and attribution are deliberately preserved.** Ellipses signal omission; no quoted material is silently harmonised.

# The case in the record's words

## Governing reading note — The substitution of documentary defensibility for genuine medicine

The patient's affirmative allegation is that the post-protest construction of the 2017 account subordinated clinical investigation to the preservation of an existing professional disposition, and that the resulting framework impaired subsequent reassessment, including recognition of possible treatment-related harm and safeguarding when suffering became intolerable. This is an allegation to test, not a finding of intention, causation or liability. [The investigative essay](journalistic-essay.html#gravity-of-case) and [the gravest allegation](index.html#gravity-statement) set out the full narrative.

This quotation-led reconstruction tests the distinction between what was done and what was represented as adequate. Read the manuscript parenthesis as a disputed inscription, not an independently dated interview entry; read the treatment-related harm and safety questions against the dated clinical record. The original quotations and source statuses remain unchanged.

## I. The clinical question was already joined together before hospital admission

The GP was not simply requesting confirmation of a Parkinsonian label. On **8 June 2017**, he expressed an interest in a specialist opinion on the patient's:

> “gait, feet and ‘muscle tension’”

The GP's April–June record and urgent referral of **29 June 2017** described painful abnormal feet, pressure pathology, toe clawing, gait change, growing muscle tone, impaired initiation of movement, loss of dexterity and occupational deterioration. By contrast, the hospital's shortened reason for referral presented a motor-focused selection:

> “39Y/O WITH DIFFICULTY INITIATING MOVEMENT, INCREASED TONE AND STIFFNESS IN ALL 4 LIMBS, COGWHEELING AT THE WRIST.”

The shortened reason omitted the explicit painful-foot, corn/callus and toe-clawing history. It also presented cogwheeling without the GP's qualification (the GP is reproduced as writing **“?Some cogwheeling at the wrist”**). This is evidence of a narrower *textual précis*, not proof that the full GP letter was unavailable to the ward or specialists.

The GP's question was therefore diagnostically integrated even though its ultimate answer was unknown. The feet were not a late complication added to an already settled neurological case. They were part of why the GP sought the assessment.

**Source trail:** [GP 2017 canonical transcription](https://github.com/TitanicParker/for-counsel/blob/main/docs/LM_2017_GP_Record_Canonical_Transcription.md); [July 2017 source comparison](https://github.com/TitanicParker/the-unanswered-referral/blob/main/JULY_2017_CLINICAL_RECORD_SOURCE_RECONSTRUCTION.md); [discharge transcription](https://github.com/TitanicParker/for-counsel/blob/main/docs/source-records/discharge-summary.html), abbreviated “Referral” section.

## II. The neurologist's assessment recovered the history the précis had omitted

The specialist's completed history describes tension going back approximately to **2004** and a hospital presentation in **2014** for physical tension affecting arms and legs. The patient's 2017 deterioration was described as the maturation of the earlier presentation. The same account recovered foot problems treated since approximately **2009–2010**, including corns, calluses and altered arches.

The handwritten interview, in the repository's provisional transcription, records:

> “Began to be aware that foot pain and leg tension weren't the same”

It separately contains the parenthetical:

> “(as the leg pain settled)”

Those two passages must be kept distinct. **Foot pain**, **leg pain**, and **leg tension** are not interchangeable. Nor does a person's report of distinguishable sensations establish that their underlying causes are independent.

The typed history subsequently says:

> “Therefore in 2012 he began to realise that the foot pain and the leg tension were not the same problem because: as the leg pain settled --- he was more aware of the tension”

That sentence adds connective and causal language to the shorter handwritten observation. The handwritten text already contained a distinction; the typed account did not invent the *existence* of difference. The question is whether the later clinical attribution gave that distinction a much stronger **causal meaning** than the patient's recorded description warranted.

**Source trail:** [manuscript comparison](https://github.com/TitanicParker/the-unanswered-referral/blob/main/JULY_2017_CLINICAL_RECORD_SOURCE_RECONSTRUCTION.md), §§3–4; [photographed interview pages](https://github.com/TitanicParker/the-unanswered-referral/blob/main/document_07_manuscript.jpg) (native visual verification outstanding); [discharge transcription](https://github.com/TitanicParker/for-counsel/blob/main/docs/source-records/discharge-summary.html), initial typed history.

## III. The specialist found a relevant movement abnormality and treated the longstanding tension

The July neurological examination did not merely note stiffness generally. It described a striking gait:

> “The gait is strikingly abnormal.”

And, more specifically:

> “He tends to walk almost with a footdrop appearance somewhat suggestive of a dystonic posture - NOT FOOT DROP.”

The examination also recorded pes planus, hammertoes, marked tone, rigidity and gross bradykinesia. These findings do **not** prove that Parkinsonism or dystonia caused every corn or lesion. They establish a particular positive motor observation that could be relevant to foot posturing, loading and pain, alongside the abnormalities of the feet themselves.

The neurologist then acted pharmacologically on the chronic tension:

> “Symptomatic treatment with clonazepam at nighttime. COMMENCED - - - he noticed a post Rx ~ sweating - ??? a Parkinsonian Dysautonomia?”

The account also recorded:

> “[developed profound sweating as InPatient transiently after nocturnal clonazepam]”

And considered the possibility that night-time procyclidine might allow clonazepam to be stopped:

> “There is an argument that if he was to tolerate nocturnal Kemadrin procyclidine dosing that it may be possible to remove the clonazepam.”

The patient's account places initiation on **Monday 3 July**, before the senior discussion early on **Tuesday 4 July**; the currently reviewed discharge narrative verifies inpatient commencement and a subsequent reaction, **not** the precise first-dose time or the exact 8:30 a.m. meeting timestamp. The consultant's clinical ownership of the tension is nevertheless clear from what he himself wrote.

**Source trail:** [July discharge transcription](https://github.com/TitanicParker/for-counsel/blob/main/docs/source-records/discharge-summary.html), examination and treatment; [six sequences and five mechanisms](https://github.com/TitanicParker/the-unanswered-referral/blob/main/THE_SIX_SEQUENCES_AND_FIVE_MECHANISMS.md), Sequence A.

## IV. The senior medication strategy did not by itself answer the feet question

The completed account explains that colleagues advised caution about starting levodopa or dopamine agonists in very young-onset disease, including concerns about dyskinesias and other longer-term complications. It describes the diagnosis as:

> “A Young Onset Parkinsonsm”

And notes a multidisciplinary review:

> “Seen at the Neurology Complex case meeting with my colleagues — likely an IPD albeit with atypical features.”

The senior treatment advice could be defensible while the original foot–movement relationship remained unresolved. A judgment about **when to introduce levodopa** is not a test of whether dystonic-looking movement, toe clawing and abnormal loading contributed to painful lesions.

One should not infer that the patient was forbidden from ever receiving levodopa: the record explicitly envisaged later introduction, and Sinemet was subsequently used. The narrower question is what assessment and follow-up were arranged for the feet while that medication choice was made.

## V. A positive motor finding stood beside an uncertain peripheral explanation

The completed Impression states:

> “The leg symptoms including corns and callouses and burning may have been completely separate or perhaps a secondary peripheral neuropathy perhaps in somewtiet respect related to the significant original alcohol intake? Not clear as this has improved.”

The transcription of the typed history also says:

> “There was no improvement off alcohol.”

The referent and timing of **“this has improved”** are not specified clearly enough to equate it with an improvement after alcohol cessation. The peripheral possibility is explicitly tentative; a diagnosis of neuropathy is not established by this passage.

In the same completed account, the consultant later enumerates reasons to suspect PRKN/PARK2—young onset, akinetic rigidity, absence of a dominant family history, symmetry and cognitive functioning—without including dystonia in that particular list. Yet the wider genetic differential refers to:

> “PARK 9 (the neuropathy question)”

An omitted sign in a non-exhaustive genetic list does not prove concealment or bad reasoning. It does raise a precise question about **relative explanatory prominence**: the neurologist's observed dystonic-looking movement was not explicitly brought into a conclusion about painful foot mechanics, while the unconfirmed peripheral hypothesis remained available as a way of characterising those symptoms.

**Source trail:** [July discharge transcription](https://github.com/TitanicParker/for-counsel/blob/main/docs/source-records/discharge-summary.html), history, Impression and genetic discussion; [positive finding/peripheral hypothesis analysis](https://github.com/TitanicParker/the-unanswered-referral/blob/main/THE_POSITIVE_FINDING_AND_THE_PERIPHERAL_HYPOTHESIS.md).

## VI. The patient's experience was made to appear more strongly separated

Immediately after the uncertain peripheral explanation, the completed Impression states:

> “However he has in his own mind - markedly divorced the feelings of tension in the legs which was/is the predominant problem -from the ‘corns and callouses’ issues and Pains in the feet”

This is the decisive attribution problem. The handwritten interview reported the patient **beginning to distinguish** foot pain from leg tension. The Impression speaks of his having **“markedly divorced”** the issues **“in his own mind.”** In context, that stronger characterisation could lead later readers to treat the proposed separation as supported by the patient's own understanding.

The same passage acknowledges continued physical pathology—“corns and callouses remain a major problem”—yet qualifies its importance by saying they were “in reality” much less of an issue. The question is how that clinician-authored evaluation was reconciled with the original GP referral, the positive dystonic-looking examination, and the patient's own unresolved concern.

The problem is not that a physician must agree with the patient about causation. It is that the author of an authoritative clinical note must distinguish **what the patient said**, **what the clinician inferred**, and **what medical evidence supported the inference**.

**Source trail:** [July documentary comparison](https://github.com/TitanicParker/the-unanswered-referral/blob/main/JULY_2017_CLINICAL_RECORD_SOURCE_RECONSTRUCTION.md), §§4–6; [discharge transcription](https://github.com/TitanicParker/for-counsel/blob/main/docs/source-records/discharge-summary.html), Impression.

## VII. The protest: the clinical state of affairs becomes an explicit object of challenge

**Analytical premise for this working version:** the patient directly protested shortly before discharge on **10 July 2017**, in the circumstances he describes, that his painful feet had not been properly investigated. This passage does not quote unexamined words from the signed supplementary statement. The testimony is a later firsthand account; witness and native record corroboration remain separate evidential tasks.

The significance of the protest is not exhausted by its absence from the completed discharge document. **It depends on the clinical state of affairs when the patient made it.** At that point, the specialist had reconstructed a roughly thirteen-year motor history, observed dystonic-looking movement, known about long-treated painful feet and deformity, commenced neurological treatment for tension, and chosen a cautious Parkinsonism medication strategy. The documented account had no identifiable integrated resolution of how abnormal neurological movement affected foot posture, loading and lesions.

That is the state the patient challenged. The objection concerned the **adequacy of the examination and disposition**, not merely a request to add a complaint entry to the chart. Accepting the event as described, the clinician had direct notice that the patient did not regard the painful-foot question as adequately investigated.

This notice does not automatically prove that discharge was unsafe or that the physician had to solve every causal question during admission. It makes the response to the objection, the adequacy of follow-up allocation, and the fidelity of the final patient-attributed account central questions.

The patient was discharged on **11 July**. The DaTscan addendum carries a transcription-finalisation time of **13 July 2017, 21:41**, and the summary was confirmed on **17 July, 19:30**, according to the repository's source chronology. This establishes **authorisation after the protest**, not that the disputed phrase was first written after it. An approved earlier interpretation and a newly rewritten interpretation have different intentional implications; both require examination of what the clinician knew at final authorisation.

**Source trail:** [recipient-and-knowledge audit](https://github.com/TitanicParker/the-unanswered-referral/blob/main/RECIPIENT_AND_KNOWLEDGE_AUDIT.md), 2017 information chain; [signed retrospective statement](https://github.com/TitanicParker/the-unanswered-referral/blob/main/Article_16_Supplementary_Statement_July_2017.pdf), **not independently text-verified in this draft**; [six propositions P3](https://github.com/TitanicParker/the-unanswered-referral/blob/main/SIX_DECISIVE_PROPOSITIONS_HOSPITAL_ANSWER.md).

## VIII. The disposition: what “As per above” adopted

The formal plan lists investigations, Parkinsonism nurse input, physiotherapy, occupational therapy, pharmacological strategy and podiatry referral. A section of the correspondence asks:

> “Please refer [Patient] to outpatient Chirodpody/Podiatry services - - we’d be grateful if you could refer please”

At the end of the longer account the final heading says:

> “Plan/Recommendations: As per above.”

The phrase is not intrinsically improper. Its significance depends on **what the preceding plan contained and omitted**. In the examined account, neurological disease was substantially investigated and a treatment pathway specified; podiatry was requested for the physical feet; the etiological relationship between abnormal motor posture and painful recurrent foot lesions remained uncertain, without an identified named pathway dedicated to resolving it.

Thus **“As per above”** can be read as the point at which the already constructed disposition was adopted for continuing management, rather than a new answer to the question the patient had just protested. That is an inference about the *effect of the document*, not proof of the author's private purpose.

The critical post-protest audit is therefore: Was the objection answered by an additional assessment, a reasoned decision that no further neurological-foot work-up was indicated, or a clearly communicated referral and return route? If so, where is it? If not, what made the existing division of responsibility adequate?

**Source trail:** [discharge transcription](https://github.com/TitanicParker/for-counsel/blob/main/docs/source-records/discharge-summary.html), plan, referrals and recommendations.

## IX. What subsequent treatment evidence did to the original question

The September 2017 specialist letter from Professor Healy states:

> “Thank you for asking me to see this man who I think probably has Park II and I would suggest testing that initially. If it is negative I would go on to test a panel.”

It also states:

> “He describes a dramatic improvement in his motor symptoms with Procyclidine low dose qds and Rasagiline 1mg od.”

The letter strengthens the young-onset motor-disorder interpretation. It does not independently determine the cause of every corn or the effects of toe posture on loading.

The treating neurologist's **24 April 2018** note records the patient's earlier two-week procyclidine withdrawal, in September/October 2017, for visual disturbance:

> “the first thing he noticed changing and changing rapidly was the return on the foot, of the outside left corn.”

The same note records that the patient felt more:

> “shifty” “unrelaxed”

and that rasagiline continued. This is **patient-reported symptom recurrence at a specific foot site during neurological medication withdrawal**, documented by the treating clinician. It is *not* objective proof that a physical corn rapidly re-formed or that only one mechanism could explain the association.

Its significance relative to the protest is precise: **the neurologist subsequently received foot-specific treatment-state evidence relevant to the very relationship the patient had challenged as uninvestigated before discharge.** The record can capture that report accurately yet leave unclear whether the earlier clinical division was reopened. That is the hypothesised record-mediated attenuation of meaning—not necessarily a failure to hear or transcribe words.

**Source trail:** [Healy's partial transcribed letter, 12 September 2017](https://github.com/TitanicParker/for-counsel/blob/main/docs/source-records/healy-response-2017-09-12.md); [24 April 2018 neurology notes](https://github.com/TitanicParker/for-counsel/blob/main/docs/source-records/neurology-notes-2018.html).

## X. The physical feet remained visible in independent clinical records

The podiatry transcription repeatedly records pathology at the **left fifth metatarsophalangeal region**. On **15 June 2018**, the objective section states:

> “Callus on L/5 MTPJ. Toes clawed 1-3 on both feet.”

Podiatry performed debridement, used toe props and insoles, and treated recurring pressure problems. This demonstrates ongoing real foot care and physical lesions, without proving a neurological origin for every lesion.

The GP's **March 2019** re-referral explicitly described feet:

> “in relating to increased tone and abnormal gait relating to Parkinson’s disease.”

And observed:

> “Parkinsons at very young age has issues with ‘clawing feet’ and pain”

The GP therefore had **not** simply accepted an absolute division between neurological movement and the feet. He continued to describe a relationship. What the reviewed sources lack is a demonstrated specialist reconciliation of that relationship with foot pressure pathology and neurological treatment effects.

**Source trail:** [Podiatry SAR transcription, including March 2019 GP re-referral](https://github.com/TitanicParker/case-evidence-ledger/blob/main/PODIATRY_SAR_2026_TRANSCRIPTION.md).

## XI. The later crisis tested the disposition's real-world adequacy

By **March 2023**, the GP's urgent neurology referral described severe bilateral foot/lower-leg pain, substantial distress and a reported wish for death. The referral described pregabalin started by the GP and a prior episode of pain improving with better Parkinsonism control. The complete two-page native referral and triage/safety documentation are not all verified here, so this paragraph is a **cross-reference**, not an exact quotation from a complete original referral.

Subsequent neurological care was not static: alternative causes were considered, medications were changed, and Sinemet was in use by **2021**, with a reported major response to controlled-release Sinemet in **March 2025**. These facts prevent an inaccurate claim that nothing was ever investigated or adjusted. The relevant question is whether ongoing care resolved or responsibly managed the specifically protested relationship between motor posture, pressure lesions and pain.

**Source trail:** [recipient-and-knowledge audit](https://github.com/TitanicParker/the-unanswered-referral/blob/main/RECIPIENT_AND_KNOWLEDGE_AUDIT.md); [25 understandings K18–K22](https://github.com/TitanicParker/the-unanswered-referral/blob/main/THE_25_ESSENTIAL_UNDERSTANDINGS.md).

## XII. What the clinician later defended, and what the hospital concluded

In his **25 March 2025** complaint response, the treating neurologist explained his longstanding view that Parkinsonism could magnify pain from other causes and defended caution about escalating dopaminergic treatment in a young patient. The reply begins its substantive clinical defence:

> “Whatever the underlying cause for the peripheral neuropathic pain symptomatologies that [Patient] has, it is our belief and understanding that [Patient] has idiopathic Parkinson’s disease.”

It also characterises pregabalin as introduced for neuropathic pain:

> “in part to try to spare further Sinemet increase”

The contemporaneous GP/nurse accounts are needed to separate **the GP's original reason for starting pregabalin** from **any later specialist intention to avoid additional Sinemet**. The clinician's treatment defence can be sincere and clinically substantive without answering why the 2017 foot–movement question was not visibly resolved or how the 2018 withdrawal observation was incorporated into an integrated explanation.

The repository describes the **27 August 2025 PAR/SIMT** review and a **3 September 2025** communication concluding:

> “no deficit in the care provided”

**Quotation provenance warning:** the brief phrase above is reproduced from the repository's documentary audit and must be checked against the original signed SIMT outcome; the **full PAR, source inventory and meeting record have not been read here**. It would be unjustified to assert that the reviewers certainly ignored the protest or manuscript. It is justified to ask where their independent assessment of the protested clinical state is shown.

## XIII. The resulting clinical and documentary question

The record's significance cannot be reduced to whether Parkinsonism was appropriately diagnosed or whether caution over levodopa was reasonable. Both may be true. Nor is it enough to show that podiatry treated the feet. It did.

The central question is what happened to the **clinical problem that existed when the patient protested**. By that point, an experienced neurological specialist had personally elicited a long motor history, observed dystonic-looking lower-limb movement, treated chronic tension, documented physical foot abnormalities and left the foot–movement causal relationship uncertain. The patient expressly objected to the adequacy of the foot investigation. The subsequently authorised account retained a strong attribution of separation to the patient and adopted the existing care plan without an identified resolution of that challenge. The years that followed supplied medication and anatomical observations relevant to the same question.

**This is the strongest currently supportable organising explanation:** an unresolved component of an integrated clinical referral was made compatible with a definite management disposition, while the final record did not visibly communicate the patient's protest against the sufficiency of that disposition. Subsequent evidence tested the original separation; the later hospital defence more clearly justified Parkinsonism prescribing than it demonstrated an independent reconstruction of the contested clinical state.

That conclusion does **not** yet establish knowing falsification, the precise avoidable portion of later suffering, or the absence of undocumented further assessment. These require native records, witness evidence and independent clinical review. But they are not needed to see why the protest—and particularly **the clinical state of affairs at the moment of protest**—is the proper centre of the case.

# Canonical working apparatus

## A. The four questions that every new source must answer

1. **Knowledge at the protest:** What did the specialist know, observe, prescribe, investigate and leave unresolved by 10 July 2017?
2. **Disposition after notice:** What happened in response to the protest, and what did the authorised discharge package carry forward to the GP and patient?
3. **Subsequent correction opportunities:** Did the Healy consultation, procyclidine dechallenge, physical podiatry record, GP's re-referral, pain escalation and later medication responses reopen the originally challenged relationship?
4. **Independent institutional assessment:** Did the 2025 PAR/SIMT actually test the original clinical state and source attribution, or chiefly review the reasonableness of treatment choices?
