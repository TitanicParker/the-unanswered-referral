> **Controlling inquiry:** What did the neurologist know; what did he decide; what did he record and omit; what did he later represent; and what did the hospital accept? The answer must be constructed by comparing those stages, not by treating any one stage as proof of the others.

## How to operate this working file

- **Evidence IDs** `EV-###` identify a source or source family; **findings** `F-###` are source-bounded observations; **propositions** `P-###` are explanations to be tested; **theories** `C-01`–`C-08` are competing or complementary cases; **investigations** `I-###` are actionable evidence tests; **decisions** `D-###` preserve choices and rejected formulations.
- **Status vocabulary:** `ESTABLISHED` (the available identified source directly establishes the stated matter); `PARTLY ESTABLISHED` (a material component is shown but the broader proposition is not); `DISPUTED` (materially contested); `AWAITING EVIDENCE` (specific needed material not presently in the inspected sources); `RESOLVED AGAINST` (evidence defeats the stated formulation, retained for audit).
- A **source may establish that a clinician wrote a proposition without establishing that proposition was medically correct**. A firsthand retrospective statement establishes the patient's account, not independent confirmation of a historical exchange. A 2025 document establishes what was represented in 2025, not that every claim about 2017 is accurate.
- **Update rule:** Never silently rewrite a conclusion. Append a dated revision in §15; preserve superseded text in a decision entry or explicitly identify the replacement and new sources. Give each new evidence item its own EV ID and adjust the affected C, P, F and I IDs.
- **Citation convention:** `[EV-04]` below points to a numbered source here and the links in §14. `[FC:HEALY17]` refers to the provenance-marked, derived 47-source corpus in [the separate For Counsel repository](https://github.com/TitanicParker/for-counsel/blob/main/sources/consolidated/evidence-corpus.md), which supplies source-line identifiers such as `HEALY17-20170912-0026`. *The original letter or email still controls.* Where the 40-document compilation and the controlled corpus overlap, they are two presentations of related material, **not independent corroborating witnesses**.
- **Earlier-analysis audit limitation:** Six requested analyses—*Beyond the Five Essays*, *The Achilles' Heel of the Hospital's Defence*, *The Extended Record*, *The Earliest Decisive Mechanism*, *The Reconstruction Inquiry*, and *From the Handwritten History to the Hospital's Defence*—were identified by title in the instruction, but exact copies under those titles were not independently located in the inspected repositories during v1.0.0. Their titles are **not** treated as verified sources. The prior [19-part philosophical analysis](THE_CASE_THREE_STATEMENTS_AND_MECHANISM.md), [case for deliberate misrepresentation](CASE_FOR_DELIBERATE_MISREPRESENTATION.md), [institutional essay](WHEN_INSTITUTIONS_BECOME_THE_AUTHORITY_ON_THEIR_OWN_ERRORS.md), the [For Counsel source–proposition matrix](https://github.com/TitanicParker/for-counsel/blob/main/evidence/SOURCE_PROPOSITION_MATRIX.md), and the numbered primary collection were available for comparison. ## 1. Current Best Explanation

### P-001 — The most explanatory supported structure

**Working conclusion, v1.0.0:** The original GP question linked painful abnormal feet and disordered movement. Hospital intake compressed that question. The neurologist's examination nevertheless included a dystonic-looking gait and foot abnormalities, while the completed synthesis offered a separate/peripheral hypothesis. His handwritten record documented the patient's *experiential* distinction; the completed account strengthened the apparent *patient-attributed* division beside the physician's uncertain hypothesis. The patient reports a 10 July protest to the treating neurologist about inadequate investigation; that protest is absent from the identified completed narrative, confirmed on 17 July. Subsequent neurological and podiatric records repeatedly supplied opportunities to re-examine the foot–movement relationship. The clinician's 2025 defence and the hospital's no-deficit outcome chiefly made treatment reasoning visible, without the disclosed material itself showing a source-by-source reconciliation of the 2017 attribution and protest.

The most powerful explanation is therefore **a two-stage discrepancy in professional representation**: **(A)** the completed 2017 account did not transparently carry forward the full referral question and the patient's contrary position; **(B)** the 2025 account of the clinical history selectively emphasized a defensible medication narrative while the fidelity of the original account remained separately contested. The two discrepancies can reinforce each other even if individual prescriptions were reasonable. This is **P-001**, not proof of fraudulent intention.

### Six-stage causal architecture

`GP's integrated problem [EV-02] → narrower intake [EV-04] → clinician's wider actual knowledge [EV-04/05/07] → provisional peripheral separation plus stronger patient attribution [EV-04] → inherited clinical framework with repeated reopening signals [EV-11–20] → reconstructed defence and institutional outcome [EV-22/29/31 + FC governance sources]`.

**The alleged 10 July protest (EV-38; FC:PSL-0003)** sits between the third and fourth stages as a possible *conscious-notice event*. If corroborated, it may transform a failure of documentation into an allegation of knowing preservation after correction. Its occurrence and importance do not require the neurologist to have acknowledged professional error: knowledge of the *objection* is distinguishable from agreement with the objection.

### Four controlling distinctions

1. **Knowing versus transmitting:** The opening hospital précis is not a complete inventory of what the neurologist knew. His own examination supplies independent knowledge of the feet. Conversely, his clinical knowledge is not identical with what the final summary communicated.
2. **Hypothesis versus corroboration:** “Not clear” is an honest qualifier to a possible neuropathy. The separate patient-attributed assertion can lend that uncertain clinical thought apparent support from another epistemic source. The critical question is whether that support was faithful.
3. **Prescribing caution versus integrated clinical inquiry:** Cautious levodopa management may have good reasons; it does not answer whether the lesion–posture–movement relationship was investigated or assigned to an appropriate clinician.
4. **Historical review versus source audit:** A PAR/SIMT judgment about the reasonableness of management is not necessarily a judgment about the truth or completeness of the patient-attributed 2017 record. The full PAR must determine whether the latter was examined.

### Scoring rule — two axes that must not be merged

**A = Present evidential strength, 0–5:** 0 unsupported; 1 patient theory only; 2 documented fragments but central link unverified; 3 substantial support with an important unresolved contradiction or missing record; 4 direct source comparison showing the primary phenomenon, even if intention/causation unproved; 5 multiple independent and converging primary sources supporting the *exact proposition scored*. **B = Consequential force if established, 0–5:** 1 local issue; 3 material reframing; 5 undermines the core source-fidelity or institutional self-review rationale. The score is **not** a probability or liability prediction.

**P-002 — Tested explanation:** The changing wording did not merely compress a clinical history. It shifted an experience report toward a proposition about the patient's medical understanding, and that proposition sat beside an uncertain separate/peripheral differential. This potentially gave a physician-origin causal partition **patient-origin corroborative force**.

**Two interpretation tests:** If “markedly divorced” was intended merely as shorthand for distinct sensations, it cannot independently corroborate medically separate causes. If it was intended to suggest the patient endorsed separate causes, ask what actual source supports that assertion. Either reading exposes the need to distinguish physician inference from patient testimony. A benign shorthand explanation is plausible *as a psychological explanation of drafting*, but it does not remove the record-fidelity issue.

**P-003 — Intent test:** A deliberate alteration requires more than the existence of the wording change. Evidence of knowledge of the actual patient position, the directional character of the changes, opportunity to correct after protest, drafting/version chronology, and the clinician's later explanations should be weighed cumulatively. If contemporary records show he accurately documented the challenge elsewhere and the summary was inadvertently incomplete, the concealment inference would materially weaken. If drafting or witness evidence shows conscious choice to preserve a false patient attribution after direct correction, it would strengthen sharply.

### Findings deliberately not conflated

- A patient can feel foot pain and tension differently without being medically certain of a shared cause.
- A clinician can legitimately entertain neuropathy without diagnostic confirmation.
- The word “burning” describes a pain quality, not a conclusive neuropathy test.
- Dystonic-looking footdrop posture is a motor sign, not alone proof of the lesion mechanism.
- Podiatry referral can be appropriate while the neurologic–mechanical question remains open.
- Deliberate falsification, clinical inadequacy and eventual avoidable physical harm are **different propositions requiring different proof**.

### The Life of the Peripheral-Neuropathy Explanation

**L-01 / Origin, July 2017:** The physician explicitly presents two alternative possibilities—completely separate foot pathology or secondary peripheral neuropathy—while saying “Not clear”; significant earlier alcohol use is also mentioned. At this point **neuropathy is a hypothesis**, not a source-demonstrated confirmed electrophysiological diagnosis. **L-02 / Attachment to patient voice:** The “in his own mind - markedly divorced” phrase is more emphatic than the handwritten sensation distinction and lends a nonclinical source of apparent support to the division. **L-03 / Operational partition and practical care:** Podiatry addresses nails, callus, clawed toes, left fifth metatarsophalangeal lesion, offloading and insole treatment. **L-07 / 2024 interventions:** Fabry-related enzyme results are reported within normal ranges; pregabalin relieves pain, but toe curling remains during standing/physical activity. Analgesic response and motor/posture response diverge. ## 8. The 10 July Protest and Documentary Closure

### Event reconstruction, without treating silence as disproof

**P-006 / Patient's account:** On Monday **10 July 2017**, the eighth day of admission and day before discharge, the patient reports four medical students at his bedside. He asked that the treating neurologist attend. He challenged having received a “full NCT” while being sent out on “bald tyres,” identifying the foot problem as unanswered. He recalls a reply to the effect that obtaining a podiatry appointment was as easy for the patient as for the neurologist. He says no further focused neurological reassessment of the foot relationship followed before discharge. This account is documented in a **12 August 2026 statement log (FC:PSL-0003)** and the **signed 2 September 2026 supplementary statement (EV-38)**; the intervening 7 July 2025 Article 15 request independently dates an earlier institutional notice of the teaching/student encounter (**FC:ART15250707**). Some earlier representations reportedly varied between 10 and 11 July; original dated versions must be retained and compared rather than silently normalized.

**The students matter as potential witnesses, not as automatic proof of event content.** Their placement/rotation records may identify who was present; individual recollections could corroborate that a protest occurred and its substance. It is also possible the teaching context generated records not in the standard clinical notes.

**The return of missing information:** The GP had already sent painful-foot pathology as part of the problem on 29 June. On this account, the patient did not invent a new complaint on 10 July. He directly restored the question that had been compressed in the hospital's intake representation. The clinical question became an institutional one: had the hospital answered what it was asked, and, if not, who would now own it?

**No duty to concede wrongdoing:** A clinician could disagree with the patient about whether enough investigation had occurred and still have a duty to take and accurately represent a material objection. Thus the knowledge potentially created by the protest is **knowledge of opposition to the clinical disposition**, not proof that the clinician acknowledged negligence.

### The author–reader–defender problem — P-008

The neurologist was the clinician who gathered the initial history and authored/confirmed the July synthesis; he later had access to his working notes and used clinical documentation in his explanations. He was therefore **not an independent corroborating historian** when he later cited the clinical history he had constructed. That does not make his defence dishonest. It identifies a structural problem in the logic of proof: a later coherent narrative produced by the original author cannot independently establish that the patient's 2017 understanding was correctly attributed.

**Best point of challenge:** compare **the full 2017 3 July handwriting** with **the specific patient-attributed words in the final impression** before discussing any moral accusation. Then ask the clinician what additional utterance supports “markedly divorced” as a statement of independent causal belief. The second challenge is his **Healy/2018/pregabalin** historical reconstruction. These tests are sharper than accusing every treatment decision of being wrongful.

### The hospital's weak point

The [signed SIMT outcome](Margaret_Tansey.html#document-31) reports reliance on notes about Sinemet risk and alternative diagnoses and says no deficit in care. It does **not**, in its disclosed reasoning, answer whether the opening intake omitted material foot information, whether the handwritten-to-typed patient attribution was faithful, or how the alleged protest was handled.

**Best institutional question:** *Which source did the PAR use to determine what the patient actually said on 3 July and protested on 10 July, and did reviewers independently compare that source with the authorised summary rather than accept the summary as the evidential baseline?* Until the full PAR is obtained, this is a targeted vulnerability rather than an established finding that no such comparison occurred.

## 12. Strategic Advice and Next Questions

### The best opening for an external reader

**Lead with the two physical source texts, not a motive accusation.** Place the **29 June GP letter beside the hospital opening précis** and **3 July manuscript beside the completed impression**. Then show the specialist's own recorded dystonic-looking gait. In the first two minutes the reader sees the original joined clinical question, the narrowed representation, the patient-observed sensory distinction, and the stronger attribution used next to the peripheral hypothesis. This is a case about **what the record actually does**, before it is a case about blame.

**Then present the 10 July testimony as a corrective event** with precise source status, the four potential witnesses, and the 17 July authorisation. Its power comes from direct notice *if corroborated*, not a demand that the clinician agree with the patient's medical judgment. Do not ask readers to assume a cover-up merely from narrative fit.

**Then place a one-page chronology of the clinician's own reopening signals:** 2017 dystonic gait and clonazepam for tension; Healy 2017 PARK II advice; 2018 PRKN plus procyclidine-associated focal corn; 2019 podiatry tone/gait; 2023 OFF dystonia and severe pain; 2024 toe curling despite pregabalin pain relief; 2025 Sinemet pain response; 2026 pathogenic PRKN variants. Let the **actual words** show that the foot–movement question had a durable evidential life independent of the complaint.

**Only then introduce the 2025 defence:** clinician's express reasons for cautious dopaminergic therapy, actual specialist advice, pregabalin provenance, and the SIMT outcome. The point is not that caution was fictitious; it is that a legitimate reason for one prescribing decision cannot independently authenticate what the patient was said to believe or prove the original foot problem was integrated.

### What should lead, what should follow, what to hold

- **Lead:** C-02 textual/documentary construction coupled with F-001/F-002; immediate reproducibility and explanatory force.
- **Hold for source foundation:** C-03 knowing preservation/concealment until students, native timestamps or equivalent witness material; use “alleged knowing distortion” candidly but not as a conceded fact. Also hold any allegation of unlawful Article 18 processing until actual controller handling is established.
- **Present as strongest longitudinal challenge:** O-02 April 2018 conjunction, plus 2019 podiatry and 2023 OFF dystonia. These are not merely generic “pain continued” anecdotes.
- **Do not lead with:** a categorical claim that no levodopa was ever prescribed until 2025 (contradicted by 2021 history); a claim that neuropathy was explicitly certified as proved in 2017 (“Not clear” says otherwise); a claim that PRKN results in 2026 retrospectively prove all 2017 foot lesions; or an accusation that all cautious prescribing was indefensible.
- **Keep safety independent:** the 2023 death-wish warning is a serious present-tense clinical concern irrespective of the contested etiological theory. It should not be absorbed as a mere rhetorical consequence of 2017.

### Most vulnerable sentences in the institutional account

1. **“No deficit in care” (EV-31):** vulnerable to being **incomplete as an answer** if review evaluated Sinemet timing but never checked patient-attributed wording, the GP referral scope, or the 10 July protest. Not conclusively refuted until full PAR obtained.
2. **“Specialist advice supported deferral” (EV-29):** vulnerable to **selective extraction** if 2017 Healy letter's low-dose dopamine replacement discussion was not reconciled with the emphasis on caution, and if the 2018 neurologist's admitted likely benefit was not linked to review triggers.
3. **“Pregabalin was introduced in part to spare Sinemet increase” (EV-22):** vulnerable to a **provenance challenge** concerning initial GP/other-doctor initiation and timing of the neurologist's stated objective.
4. **“Patient markedly divorced” (EV-04):** directly vulnerable to **manuscript-to-summary fidelity testing**, which lies logically prior to medication disputes.

### Three questions with highest investigative return

1. **What exact evidence supports the neurologist attributing a strongly separate medical understanding to this patient, and when was that sentence drafted and approved?** (I-02/I-03)
2. **What did the PAR actually compare with the completed 2017 account, and was the 10 July event/July-August 2025 data dispute visible to those who declared no deficit?** (I-15/I-16)
3. **When did the neurologist first connect—or explicitly rule out connecting—the recurring left-foot pressure lesion to abnormal posturing and motor-medication state, especially after the 2018 procyclidine event?** (I-08/I-06/I-09)

### The strongest bridge from July 2017 to 2025

The bridge is **not simply “peripheral neuropathy was repeated for eight years.”** That would flatten genuine later nuance and cause the defender to win by pointing out that neuropathy remained only possible. The stronger bridge is **a continuing separation in the allocation of explanatory responsibility**. The clinician could regard neuropathy as possible, observe dystonia and medication-sensitive pain, and treat different symptoms, while no one clearly owned the neurological–mechanical relationship that had motivated the GP's referral. In 2025, the historical defence could accurately explain cautious Sinemet prescribing while failing to establish that this original relationship was investigated and that the patient's 2017 testimony had been accurately represented.

**This mechanism can be true even if** some foot pain had a peripheral component, Parkinsonism management was partly appropriate, and the patient later changed his own explanations of symptoms. It would be *disproved or materially weakened* by a contemporaneous integrated neurological–podiatric assessment that credibly resolved or allocated the connection, and by native evidence supporting the actual patient-attributed wording.
